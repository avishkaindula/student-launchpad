# CV Guide for Software Engineering Students

A recruiter spends **6–10 seconds** on a first pass. Your CV must make the right three things obvious
in that time: what you can build, proof that you've built it, and how to reach you.

## Structure (one page until you have ~3 years of experience)

1. **Header:** name, role you want ("Software Engineering Intern"), city, phone, email, LinkedIn, GitHub, portfolio.
2. **Summary (optional, 2–3 lines):** what you build and what you're looking for. Skip clichés.
3. **Skills:** grouped, honest, no rating bars. *Languages · Frameworks · Data · Cloud/Tools.*
4. **Experience:** internships, part-time work, freelance, serious volunteer tech roles.
5. **Projects:** 3–4 of your best, each with a link. For students this is often the most important section.
6. **Awards & competitions:** hackathons, coding competitions, scholarships.
7. **Education:** degree, university, expected graduation, relevant modules or results if strong.
8. **Certifications** (only relevant ones) and **languages** (spoken).

Put your strongest section near the top. No internship yet? Put **Projects** above Experience.

## Writing bullet points that land

**Formula:** *Action verb* + *what you built or did* + *how / with what* + *result (a number if possible).*

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Worked on the backend. | Built a REST API in NestJS + PostgreSQL serving 12 endpoints for 300+ beta users. |
| Responsible for the mobile app. | Led development of a React Native app (iOS & Android) used by 800+ staff; reached #21 in its App Store category. |
| Used AI in the project. | Implemented a retrieval-augmented chatbot (Next.js, AI SDK) that answers policy questions with cited sources. |
| Participated in a hackathon. | Won 1st place of 40 teams at X Hackathon by building a multi-agent research tool in 24h. |

**Numbers to look for:** users, response time, % faster, hours saved, team size you led, test count,
ranking, downloads. No numbers? Use scope ("end-to-end", "solo", "led a team of 5").

## Project descriptions

```
Project Name — one-line description                         [GitHub] [Live demo]
• Problem it solves and for whom (1 line)
• What YOU built, with the key technologies
• Proof: users, result, award, or the hardest technical problem you solved
```

Group project? Say what **you** did: *"Owned the payments module and CI pipeline in a 6-person team."*

## Honesty rules

- Only list skills you could be interviewed on tomorrow. Interviewers pick from your CV.
- Don't inflate titles. "Lead" means you actually led people or the project.
- Group work must be described as group work.
- AI can help you *edit* your CV, but every claim must be true and in your own voice.

## Formatting that survives ATS (applicant tracking systems)

- Simple, single-column layout. Standard headings ("Experience", "Projects", "Education").
- No text inside images, tables or text boxes for important content. No photo (unless the job market expects it).
- A clean font (Inter, Calibri, Arial, Helvetica) at 10–11pt. Consistent dates (`Jan 2025 – Present`).
- Export as **PDF** named `Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf`.
- Tools: Google Docs, or the widely used "Jake's Resume" LaTeX template on Overleaf.

## Review checklist (use it on your own CV, or on a friend's)

- [ ] One page; nothing important below the fold.
- [ ] Links work and point to *pinned, documented* repos (see [GitHub profile guide](05-github-profile.md)).
- [ ] Every bullet starts with a strong verb and shows a result or scope.
- [ ] Projects say what *you* did.
- [ ] No spelling mistakes (read it aloud, or backwards).
- [ ] Skills match the job description you're applying for (tailor it per application).
- [ ] Email address is professional; phone number has the country code.
- [ ] Saved as PDF with a sensible file name.
