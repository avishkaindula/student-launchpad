# The One-Minute Introduction

"Tell me about yourself" is the first question in almost every interview, networking chat and
mentoring session. It's an elevator pitch, not your life story. Aim for **45–60 seconds**.

## Structure: Present → Past → Future

| Part | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | 15 s | Who you are now and what you focus on |
| **Past** | 25–30 s | 1–2 proof points: a project, an internship, a win — with a result |
| **Future** | 10–15 s | What you're looking for and why *this* company/role |

## Example

> "I'm a second-year Computer Science student at IIT, and I enjoy building full-stack web apps —
> especially ones with a real user at the other end.
>
> Last semester my team built a room-booking system for our university that prevents double bookings
> at the database level; I owned the booking engine and its test suite. Before that I built a React
> Native app for a local café chain that's now used by around 800 staff.
>
> I'm now looking for a software engineering internship where I can work on production code with a
> strong team — and your work on <specific product> is exactly the kind of problem I'd like to learn from."

## Tips

- **Write it, then say it out loud** 5 times. Cut anything you stumble on.
- **Lead with what's relevant** to the listener: AI work for an AI company, mobile for a mobile team.
- **One number beats three adjectives.** "800 staff use it" > "a scalable, robust, innovative app".
- **End with a hook** that invites the next question ("…happy to walk through how we prevented double bookings").
- **Avoid:** reading your CV aloud, personal details (age, family), "I'm a hard worker and a team player".

## Feedback checklist (for mock sessions)

- [ ] Under 60 seconds?
- [ ] Clear about who they are and what they want?
- [ ] At least one concrete proof point with a result?
- [ ] Tailored to the listener?
- [ ] Natural delivery: eye contact, pace, no filler-word storm ("like", "basically", "actually")?
